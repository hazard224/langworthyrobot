Step 3 · Witness statements (completely made up)

From the people who tried

In fairness to the possible human

His side of it

His office says he has held a telephone town hall every month since taking office, reaching more than 100,000 constituents across all nine counties on a typical call. He has called the push for in-person events an organized campaign by national activist groups.

Phone calls count for something. But a town hall you can't walk into, look someone in the eye at, or hand a casserole to is a conference call.

Here's ours

If he really cared, he'd be here. In the fire halls, the diners, the barns, the church basements. He'd know what's actually happening in the Southern Tier and how people actually feel about it. You don't learn that from a call screener in Washington.

But he's got a comfortable job in D.C., and in a district rated R+10 he figures the R after his name does the campaigning for him. Why drive to Wellsville when the letter does the work?

Meanwhile, farmers are struggling. Small businesses are struggling. And the hospitals that keep these towns alive are bracing for cuts he voted for.

Rural hospitals

He took a dollar.
He handed back 36 cents.

He championed the 2025 "One Big Beautiful Bill" on the House floor and voted for it. KFF estimates it cuts federal Medicaid spending in rural America by about $137 billion over ten years. The "fix" he points to is a $50 billion rural health fund, and only a small share of that can go straight to hospitals and clinics for care.

His floor speech · FactCheck.org · Georgetown CCF

Groceries

$186 billion off the kitchen table.

The same bill cut SNAP by about $186 billion over ten years. It extends work requirements to people up to age 64, and starting in 2028 it pushes part of the cost onto states like New York. That money doesn't only feed families. It gets spent at local grocers, farm stands and farmers markets.

Congressional Research Service · Supermarket News

Health insurance

17 Republicans voted to lower your premiums. He wasn't one.

The enhanced ACA tax credits that kept marketplace premiums down expired at the end of 2025. In January 2026 the House voted 230–196 to bring them back for three years. Seventeen Republicans crossed over, including three from New York: Garbarino, LaLota and Lawler. Langworthy wasn't on the list.

Axios: the 17 Republicans · Ballotpedia

Local news

He cheered cutting the station that covers him.

He backed the 2025 rescission package that wiped out $1.1 billion for the Corporation for Public Broadcasting, calling it "restoring fiscal sanity." WSKG, the Southern Tier's public radio and TV station, was getting about $1.3 million a year from CPB. CPB announced it was shutting down weeks after the cut passed.

WSKG on his support · CPB shutting down

The Epstein files

When it counted, he voted to keep them shut.

In July 2025, Democrats on the House Rules Committee tried to force a floor vote on releasing the Epstein files. Langworthy sits on that committee, and he voted to block it. Four months later, after the public pressure became overwhelming, the House passed the release bill 427–1 and he voted yes along with almost everyone else. Being the 427th vote isn't leadership. The vote that mattered was the one in July.

Newsweek: who blocked it · News10: Langworthy defends his no vote · NPR: the 427–1 vote
